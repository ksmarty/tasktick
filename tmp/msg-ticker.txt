Wake the reminder ticker on the minute instead of a minute after boot

The ticker was a bare `setInterval(..., 60_000)`, so its phase was whatever
moment the process happened to start: a reminder set for 09:00 could be
announced at 09:00:47, and nothing about that looked wrong from inside the app.

A reminder's fire time is a minute on the clock, so the wake-up is scheduled
against the clock now: a self-arming `setTimeout` to the next minute boundary
plus `REMINDER_SETTLE_MS` (250 ms), recomputed from the moment each tick actually
fires so a late wake-up re-aligns instead of walking the phase further off every
minute.

The quarter second is not slack in delivery. It is what stops a `setTimeout` that
fires a hair early from asking "what is due now" two milliseconds before the
minute and leaving the reminder for the next tick — a whole minute later. It
covers every path at once: the app's own Apprise and Web Push, and the Home
Assistant webhook the integration registers.

`tests/reminder-scheduler.test.ts` pins the arithmetic against a frozen clock —
from 09:00:37.412 the first sweep is 22_838 ms away, at 09:01:00.250 — that
nothing runs before the minute, that ticks stay a minute apart, and that a
five-second-late wake-up re-aligns rather than drifting.

The service worker is bumped because `src/` changed, and AGENTS.md's description
of the ticker now says when it wakes and why.
